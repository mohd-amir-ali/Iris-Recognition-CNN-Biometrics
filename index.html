Biometric Iris Recognition via Convolutional Neural Networks.

The human iris contains over 240 unique trabecular degrees of freedom (crypts, radial furrows, and collarette rings) that remain immutable throughout life. This system isolates the circular iris from Near-Infrared (850nm) eye scans using the OpenCV Hough Circle Transform and classifies identities across 108 subjects with a 7-layer Deep Sequential CNN.

STAGE 01 - PREPROCESSING

5×5 Median Blur & Hough Circles

Raw grayscale eye scans undergo a 5×5 median filter (cv2.medianBlur) before cv2.HoughCircles detects the circular iris boundary (param1=63, param2=70) and masks out eyelids and eyelashes.

gray = cv2.medianBlur(cv2.imread(path, 0), 5) ; circles = cv2.HoughCircles(gray, HOUGH_GRADIENT, 1, 20, param1=63, param2=70) ; roi = cv2.bitwise_and(img, mask)
STAGE 02 - FEATURE LEARNING

Dual Conv2D + MaxPooling

Two successive 32-filter 3×3 Convolution layers with ReLU activation and 2×2 MaxPooling extract spatial Gabor-like iris crypts, radial furrows, and collarette textures.

x = MaxPool2D(2,2)(ReLU(Conv2D(32, (3,3))(input_64x64x3))) ; x = MaxPool2D(2,2)(ReLU(Conv2D(32, (3,3))(x))) ; flat = Flatten()(x)
STAGE 03 - IDENTITY MATCHING

256-D Embedding & Softmax

Flattened feature maps pass through a 256-unit dense representation layer into a 108-way Softmax classifier, identifying the exact enrolled person in under 18 milliseconds.

emb_256 = ReLU(Dense(256)(flat)) ; probs_108 = Softmax(Dense(108)(emb_256)) ; subject_id = argmax(probs_108) + 1
